    Field notes for the board exam

    Check

    Sanity-check every answer

    Apparent dip is never bigger than true dip. It equals true dip only on a section perpendicular to strike, and drops to zero along strike. If your α comes out larger than δ, you used the wrong angle.

    α ≤ δ, always
    Apparent dip

    Know which angle β is

    β is measured from the strike line. If a problem gives the angle from the dip direction instead (call it θ), use cos θ instead of sin β. Same answer, different trig function.

    tan α = tan δ × sin β
    Notation

    Convert bearings to azimuth first

    N35E is 035°, S20W is 200°, N70W is 290°. Subtracting azimuths avoids most quadrant mistakes. Then fold the difference into 0–90° to get β.

    S20°W = 180° + 20° = 200°
    Notation

    Lock the notation

    Write strike with the right-hand rule: dip is 90° clockwise from strike. So 035/40 always means dipping toward 125° (SE). Writing the dip quadrant letter as well (40 SE) is still a good habit.

    dip direction = strike + 90°
    Method

    One picture solves them all

    Every guide here uses the same trick: imagine the bed one depth h deeper. That deeper line runs parallel to strike. True dip is the shortest walk to it; apparent dip is a longer, slanted walk.

    tan (dip) = h ÷ map distance
    Three-point

    Three-point recipe

    Label the points high, middle and low. On the high–low line, find the spot at the middle elevation by simple ratio. Join it to the middle point: that line is strike. Dip is the elevation drop over the perpendicular distance.

    tan δ = Δz ÷ d
    Apparent dip

    Mind the plunge direction

    A section line has two ends. The apparent dip plunges toward the end on the down-dip side of strike. Always state it, as in 34.5° → 090°.

    ends: T and T ± 180°
    Thickness

    Thickness: flat ground first

    On flat ground with a traverse square to strike, use the simple form. Only add the Δz·cos δ term when the ground slopes, and only use the width measured at right angles to strike.

    t = w × sin δ
    Drillhole

    Drillhole: draw the dip section

    Measure the collar's distance from the outcrop's strike line in the dip direction. Distance along strike never changes the depth.

    V = x × tan δ
    Stereonet

    Stereonet habits

    Always rotate the tracing paper, never the net. Dips and plunges are counted in from the rim along a straight E–W or N–S line. Poles plot 90° away from the great circle, on the opposite side.

    pole plunge = 90° − δ
    Apparent dip

    Memorize the curve shape

    Sweep the slider in the Apparent dip tab. For moderate dips, α stays close to δ until β drops below about 45°, then falls quickly. This lets you eyeball multiple-choice answers.

    β → 90° gives α → δ
    Exam habit

    Sketch it in ten seconds

    Before any calculation, draw a quick compass on scratch paper with the strike line and a dip arrow. It tells you which quadrant the answer must be in, so you can strike out impossible choices first.

    sketch → estimate → solve
